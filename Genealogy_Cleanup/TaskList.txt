Going from 4,500 down to 600 records is an ~87% reduction. That is a massive contraction, and it strongly suggests that the pruning either aggressively severed collateral in-law branches early, or hit bottleneck "bridge" individuals whose removal cascaded through entire subtrees.

The intermediate artifacts generated at each stage of the pipeline allow you to audit what was discarded and why:

**1. Duplication vs. Structural Loss (`quarantine_catalog.csv`)**

* Check `quarantine_catalog.csv` sorted by `Subtree_Size` descending.


* Look for duplicate branches where `Subtree_Size` is unusually large. If a duplicate couple was severed, all descendants exclusively under that couple were quarantined. Ensure that the `Primary_Anchor` in your canonical file actually carries those descendants or that you didn't quarantine a branch with richer generational data.



**2. Collateral & Surnames (`branches_to_cull.csv`)**

* Open `branches_to_cull.csv` and filter by `Cull_Decision == "PRUNE"`.


* Verify whether people with maternal ancestral lines (e.g., your direct grandmothers' ancestral families) were classified as `PRUNE`.


* Remember that `gedcom_tuned_branches.py` only traces **ancestors of root** and **all descendants of those ancestors**. It treats the ancestors of an in-law (or the parents of a spouse who is not a direct ancestor) as extraneous collateral. If you want to retain allied local families, their surnames must be added to `SURNAME_TARGETS`.



**3. Gateway Bottlenecks (`gedcom_find_bridges.py`)**

* Run `gedcom_find_bridges.py <file.ged> <root_id> 5`.


* Setting the cutoff to 5 or 6 hops will highlight the exact gateway individuals where hundreds of people branch off. If one of those gateways is marked `PRUNE` in your culling sheet, pruning that single person or couple will discard the entire downstream subtree.



**Strategy for Your Friend's 12,000-Person File**
Do not run the full quarantine/prune pipeline right away. Run diagnostics first:

1. **Baseline audit:** Run `gedcom_audit.py` to check INDI/FAM ratios and custom tags.


2. **Duplicate scan:** Run `gedcom_detect_duplicates.py` and inspect `duplicates_manifest.csv`. Large trees assembled from online merges often have hundreds of duplicate sibling pairs and duplicate couples.


3. **Distance distribution:** Run `gedcom_distance_report.py` using his root ID to examine how many records sit at 10+ hops or are completely disconnected (`-1`).
